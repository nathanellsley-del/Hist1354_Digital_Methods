Dataset descriptor: 

Published on Data.gov, this data was curated by the United States Department of Veterans Affairs' Office of Enterprise Integration to publicly report the verified demographical and statistical information of Vietnam era veterans, separating the data by state (“Vietnam Veterans by State:  2014”). The data is owned and published by the Department’s Office of Information and Technology as part of its Open Data Initiative which aims to promote government transparency by providing publicly accessible information relating to veterans (“Department of Veterans Affairs Open Data Portal”). 

In this file, each row represents the data that a state and/or district collected and reported for the Vietnam veterans that originated from that state (“Vietnam Veterans by State:  2014”). Each column compares the number of, or percent make-up of, various gender, racial, financial, age, and educational variables which were recorded and published by each state (“Vietnam Veterans by State:  2014”). Certain data points aren’t reported in which case the population corresponding to those data points are statistically insignificant. (“Vietnam Veterans by State:  2014”). 

Although this dataset is extensive, its variables set parameters that inherently might exclude some servicemembers. For example, by labelling the races of service members as only White or African American, the data implies that everyone who served was one of those two races thereby disenfranchising the service of Hispanics, Native Americans, and other minorities. Additionally, this dataset only deals with statistics surrounding quantitative variables which are not able to paint a picture of the lived experience of servicemembers. Because this dataset does not include important qualitative factors such as the severity of combat experienced, the personal attitudes of servicemembers towards the war, and status of lives post-military service (ie. homeless, incarcerated, etc.) All of these factors play an important role in crafting an analysis of the current experiences of Vietnam War veterans after the war yet are not included in the dataset. Therefore, solely relying on this dataset to draw conclusions about the realities of Vietnam War veterans in the United States without any other research would prove incomplete and potentially misleading.
